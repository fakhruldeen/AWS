# 05. Cloud Economics & FinOps Prerequisite

## 1. Capital Expenditure (CapEx) vs. Operational Expenditure (OpEx)

| Dimension | Capital Expenditure (CapEx) | Operational Expenditure (OpEx) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accounting Model** | Upfront investment in physical assets | Ongoing operational expenses as incurred |
| **Tax Treatment** | Depreciated over 3 to 7 years | Deducted in the current tax year |
| **Capacity Planning** | Must forecast peak demand years ahead | Elastic provisioning matching actual demand |
| **Risk** | High risk of over-provisioning or running out of capacity | Low financial commitment; pay for what you consume |
| **AWS Analogy** | Traditional on-premises physical data center | AWS Cloud on-demand consumption model |

## 2. Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)
TCO includes both direct and indirect costs:
- **Direct Costs:** Hardware, networking cables, storage arrays, software licenses, power, cooling, datacenter floor space.
- **Indirect Costs:** Real estate leases, physical security guards, hardware maintenance, network engineering payroll, disaster recovery site upkeep, opportunity costs of slow time-to-market.

## 3. The 6 Core Financial Advantages of AWS Cloud
1. **Trade fixed capital expense for variable expense:** Pay only for computing capacity consumed.
2. **Benefit from massive economies of scale:** AWS aggregates millions of customers to negotiate lower hardware and power prices, continually lowering costs.
3. **Stop guessing capacity:** Eliminate underutilized idle servers or catastrophic site outages during demand spikes.
4. **Increase speed and agility:** Spin up thousands of servers globally within minutes instead of waiting months.
5. **Stop spending money running and maintaining data centers:** Focus engineering resources on core business differentiators.
6. **Go global in minutes:** Deploy applications worldwide across multiple AWS Regions with single-digit millisecond latency.
